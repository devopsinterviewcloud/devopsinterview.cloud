Nearly every startup chasing enterprise deals runs into this question before they've even picked an auditor. Interviewers ask it because the wrong choice wastes months of budget and still fails to unblock the deal it was meant to close.

## The answer interviewers want

Type I and Type II both test the same SOC2 controls, but they differ in what they prove.

Type I is a snapshot. It confirms your controls are designed correctly and in place at one specific point in time. It's faster and cheaper, often a matter of weeks once you're ready, though fees swing widely with scope and auditor.

Type II goes further. It tests whether those same controls actually operated effectively over a window of time, usually 6-12 months. That takes real evidence: logs, tickets, access reviews, change records, collected continuously, not just a policy doc shown once. It costs more and takes months, since you can't compress the observation period: first reports often use a three-month window, later ones six to twelve.

The practical difference in a sales context: enterprise customers usually require Type II before they'll sign. A Type I report shows your controls are designed and in place on one date; it doesn't show they held up under daily operation. Security teams on the buyer side know this and will ask for Type II specifically.

So the guidance is simple. If you're pre-revenue or very early and just need something to point to while you build real evidence collection, start with Type I. Otherwise, go straight for Type II, because that's what actually unblocks enterprise contracts. Doing Type I first and then Type II back to back can mean paying for two audits instead of budgeting once for the one that matters.

## Why interviewers ask this

This question screens for whether you understand compliance as a business and sales function, not just a checkbox. Anyone can define SOC2; fewer candidates can explain why picking the wrong audit type burns budget and still fails to close deals. It also signals whether you've actually worked with auditors and sales teams on this, versus just reading the acronym somewhere.

## Follow-ups to expect

**How do you prepare evidence collection for a Type II audit?** You need continuous, automated evidence: access logs, change management records, incident tickets, collected across the full observation window, not assembled retroactively.

**What happens if you fail a Type II audit midway through the observation period?** SOC2 isn't really pass or fail midway. The auditor reports the exception and weighs compensating controls; a serious one can mean a qualified opinion. Fix the gap, document it, and show it working for the rest of the period.

**Why would a company ever choose Type I if Type II is what enterprise customers want?** Mainly cost and timeline control for pre-revenue companies that need a credible interim artifact while they build out the evidence pipeline Type II requires.

## The common wrong answer

Candidates often describe Type I and Type II as just "shorter audit vs longer audit" without connecting it to cost, evidence requirements, or the sales motion. Some also default to recommending Type I as the safe first step for every company, missing that it can mean paying for two audits when the customer was always going to demand Type II.
